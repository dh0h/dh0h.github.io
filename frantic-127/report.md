## Text as published

Startup-credit offers are easy to misread when the headline is the first fact you notice. The useful question is not only how large the discount is; it is whether your company can document the eligibility conditions and reach the correct application path.

Before opening a form, I build a five-field record: benefit, eligibility evidence, exclusions, application route, and evidence date. Here is what that audit looks like with three real offers. The records make comparison easier, but the vendor’s current terms control.

## 1. Separate the maximum from what you can expect

The [Sourcey Kiro Pro+ record](https://sourcey.com/c/kiro/offers/one-year-kiro-pro-plus) is based on vendor pages read on August 7, 2026. It lists up to one year of credits, with packages for up to 2, 10, or 30 users depending on the tier. Kiro’s [current startup page](https://kiro.dev/startups/) still shows those three limits and says active AWS Activate credits make a startup ineligible for this promotion.

Write the benefit line as “maximum: one year; tier: selected package; approval: vendor decision”—not “we get one year.” The distinction matters for budgeting: a maximum benefit describes the ceiling, not an award every applicant receives.

## 2. Turn eligibility into a proof list

The same dated Kiro record lists early-stage through Series A, venture backing, a valid AWS Account ID, and an official business email matching both the startup domain and AWS primary development account. It also says the applicant must not currently participate in AWS Activate, be at least 18, live in a supported location, and apply during the promotion window. Kiro’s [terms](https://kiro.dev/startups/terms) specify the current promotion period as April 7 through December 31, 2026.

Before applying, gather four items: company stage and funding evidence; the AWS Account ID; confirmation that the account email matches the company domain; and confirmation that the company does not have active AWS Activate credits. Then check the applicant’s age, country, and timing against the terms. These are not all self-certifications: Kiro reserves the approval decision, so a completed checklist is not a guarantee.

## 3. Keep neighboring programs on separate lines

The [Sourcey AWS Activate Portfolio record](https://sourcey.com/c/aws/offers/aws-activate-portfolio-credits), observed on August 4, 2026, lists up to $200,000 for pre-Series B startups with an AWS Activate Provider Org ID. AWS’s [current credits page](https://aws.amazon.com/startups/credits/) likewise lists up to $200,000 for Portfolio and requires that provider ID. Its general eligibility also includes being founded within the past 10 years, having a paid-tier AWS account, and being new to Activate credits or requesting more than previously received.

That is a different application route from Kiro. Keep the exact tier and provider identifier beside the amount; an AWS Account ID is not an Activate Provider Org ID, and eligibility for one offer does not establish eligibility for the other.

## 4. Read the exclusions and the post-promotion price

The [Sourcey ActiveCampaign Incubator record](https://sourcey.com/c/activecampaign/offers/activecampaign-incubator-program) reflects a vendor-page read dated July 27, 2026. It lists 90% off an annual plan for the first year, for a new customer under two years old who is currently a partner in an incubator or an incubator alum within the prior 12 months and has raised no more than $1 million.

I rechecked ActiveCampaign’s [official startup page](https://www.activecampaign.com/start-up): it states those eligibility limits, says approval is at ActiveCampaign’s discretion, and notes that the account is charged full price after the first year. That last term changes the cost calculation. A discount percentage without its duration and renewal price is not enough to compare the offer.

## 5. Make the record auditable

For each offer, save the maximum benefit, the exact eligibility wording, what evidence the company can supply, each exclusion, the application URL, and two dates: when the Sourcey record says its vendor evidence was observed, and when you last checked the vendor page. Those dates are not interchangeable. In these examples, the Sourcey evidence dates range from July 27 to August 7, while I checked the vendor pages on October 6, 2026.

If a condition is discretionary, label it “vendor decides” instead of marking it as passed. If a page has changed, follow the vendor’s latest terms and note the difference. The resulting application should be a set of verified company facts—not a guess based on the largest number on a landing page.
