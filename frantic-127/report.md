## Text as published

Short answer: start with the Kiro-specific application page, not with a generic list of AWS credits. The offer has its own conditions, and approval is not automatic.

I checked the public Kiro and AWS records on August 28, 2026. The current Sourcey record for Kiro's one-year Pro+ offer says that qualifying venture-backed startups from early stage through Series A can receive up to one year of Kiro Pro+ at no cost. The packages cover up to 2, 10, or 30 users depending on the tier. That is a useful starting point, but it is not the same thing as a promise that every startup gets the largest package.

Here is the application path I would use:

1. Check the Kiro startup application page on AWS, then read the Kiro startup terms. Use the vendor pages as the source of record for the current form, documents, and final decision.

2. Confirm the basic company conditions before spending time on the form. The Sourcey record says the company must be an early-stage through Series A startup and venture-backed. The application also requires a valid AWS Account ID and an official business email that matches both the startup domain and the AWS primary development account.

3. Check the exclusions carefully. The record says the applicant must not currently participate in AWS Activate, must be at least 18, and must live in a supported country. The listed exclusions include China and Greater China, along with several other countries and territories. If your company or account falls into an excluded region, the sensible answer is to stop there rather than assume that a public application form means eligibility.

4. Check the date window. The current record describes an April 7 through December 31, 2026 promotion period, followed by Kiro approval. I would save a copy of the terms or at least record the URL and the date checked, because startup offers change while old articles remain searchable.

5. Submit the application with the company details that match the AWS account. If the application asks you to choose a package, treat the user count as a practical limit, not as a guaranteed entitlement. The offer record describes Starter, Growth, and Scale packages, and Kiro decides whether the application is approved.

It also helps to separate Kiro credits from AWS Activate credits. The current Sourcey record for AWS Activate Portfolio credits, checked August 28, 2026, lists a separate offer that goes up to $200,000 for pre-Series B startups that apply with an AWS Activate Provider Org ID. The broader AWS Activate record also lists a Founders offer that starts at $1,000, with selected participants eligible for up to $5,000. Those are different eligibility lanes, so having an AWS account or applying for one program does not automatically unlock the others.

The same check prevents exaggerated claims about other startup deals. For example, the Sourcey record for ActiveCampaign's Incubator Program, also checked August 28, 2026, records 90% off an annual plan for the first year. It is limited to new customers under two years old who are in an incubator or graduated from one within the previous 12 months, with no more than $1,000,000 in funding. The headline percentage is real, but it only makes sense alongside those conditions.

My rule is simple: use a catalog to find the offer, then use the vendor's current page to decide whether you can apply. Record the benefit, the eligibility test, the access URL, the terms URL, and the date you checked. Do not treat an "up to" amount as a guaranteed award, and do not treat a public form as proof that the vendor will approve the application.
